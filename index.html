03 / THE ENGINEER

Building with purpose.

That question led me to data engineering. Today I build systems that organize, move, and make data useful.

Read my full story ＋

Funny enough, I wasn't always someone who loved coding.

Growing up, I was a pretty good student. Things went smoothly until 12th grade, when mathematics started giving me a hard time. I barely managed to pass, and when I later joined college to study Computer Science Engineering, I found myself facing another challenge: programming.

I still remember learning C, my first programming language. Even writing a simple program felt difficult. There were moments when I thought about giving up. But I knew how much my parents had invested in my education, and I didn't want their sacrifices to go to waste.

So I kept going. I spent countless hours practicing, making mistakes, and trying to understand why things worked. Somewhere along the way, something clicked: programming wasn't about memorizing code. It was about understanding the logic behind it.

Once I started looking at it that way, coding became less intimidating. Slowly, I began to enjoy it. I participated in coding competitions, won a few prizes, and secured a job through campus placements.

During my third year of engineering, one question changed my direction: “The world generates so much data every day. Where does all of it go?”

That curiosity led me to databases, data systems, and eventually data engineering. Before graduating, I'd landed my first role as a Data Engineer. Today, I continue that journey, building data systems and learning something new along the way.

You don't have to be good at something from the beginning. Sometimes you just have to stay curious long enough to discover you love it.
